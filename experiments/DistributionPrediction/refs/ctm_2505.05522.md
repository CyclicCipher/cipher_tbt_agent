# Continuous Thought Machines (Sakana AI, 2025) — what it teaches about the usefulness of time

Darlow, Regan, Risi, Seely & Jones, *Continuous Thought Machines*, arXiv 2505.05522v3 (28 May 2025), 59 pages; PDF in
`docs/extra papers/ctm.pdf`. **Read 2026-10-08:** §1–§13 (method, ImageNet, mazes, CIFAR, sorting, parity, Q&A MNIST,
RL, discussion, limitations); appendices only searched. Read with the user's framing (2026-10-08): this paper speaks to
the usefulness of time, not to how to build distribution prediction itself.

## The mechanism, briefly

- **An internal time axis decoupled from the data:** "internal ticks" t = 1..T over which the model thinks, even about
  a static image or maze. (Our NTA "depth axis", with more structure per tick.)
- **Neuron-level models:** each of the D neurons has its OWN small MLP that reads that neuron's last M pre-activations
  (M ≈ 10–100) — a private filter over its own recent history — instead of a fixed activation function.
- **Synchronization as the representation:** the model is read out, and forms its attention queries, from
  S = Z·Zᵀ — the inner products of neurons' activity HISTORIES over the ticks so far (sub-sampled neuron pairs, each with
  a learned exponential decay that sets how far back it looks) — not from the state at one tick.
- **Loss across ticks:** per example, the average of the loss at the tick with the LOWEST loss and at the tick with the
  HIGHEST certainty (1 − normalised entropy). Adaptive compute comes out of this with no extra halting loss.
- Trained by ordinary gradient descent through all ticks.

## What it shows about time — six lessons, with the evidence

1. **Internal time lets a fixed network run sequential algorithms on static inputs.** Cumulative parity of 64 bits
   given all at once: accuracy rises with ticks; 75–100 ticks reach 100% in some seeds, while parameter-matched LSTMs
   top out at 67% (10 ticks) and become unstable with more. The attention visibly walks the sequence — forward with 100
   ticks, BACKWARD with 75 (two different algorithms found). Sorting: the wait between outputs grows with the gap between
   successive values — the timing carries the algorithm.

2. **Time can replace positional encodings: the model builds its own map by moving.** The maze model has NO positional
   embedding and must output the route as 100 moves; its attention steps along the path, keeps tracing it when run for
   2× the trained ticks, and, re-applied from where it stopped, solves 39×39 mazes of any route length nearly perfectly
   (accuracy falls on 99×99). The best LSTM fails beyond ~20 steps; a feed-forward model overfits. **This directly
   supports the notes' "we must not hard-code the dimensions":** with an internal clock and attention it can steer, a
   model can do without a coordinate scheme, building its spatial representation from its own movement.

3. **Represent a trajectory by its statistics, not by a snapshot.** Their first design read out the state at a tick
   and failed: the model learned WHEN the loss was applied instead of organising itself, and the oscillating neurons made
   it unstable. Reading out how neurons co-vary over time — a time-independent second-order statistic — fixed both
   (§12.4). (My note: this is close to the notes' own move — the representation is a summary of a DISTRIBUTION of
   activity over time, not a point.)

4. **Timing is a memory.** Q&A MNIST: digits are shown, then operations; the digits lie OUTSIDE the neuron-level
   history window when the answer is due, yet with 10 ticks per input all three seeds exceed 96% on the hardest
   in-distribution case (4 digits, 4 operations); the 10-tick LSTM stays at or below 21%. Information is held in how
   neurons are synchronised, not in a stored vector.

5. **Timescales are learned, and used only where the task is sequential.** The per-pair decays (how far back a pair
   looks) stayed near zero — long memory — for all but 3 of 8,196 pairs on ImageNet, and became meaningful for ~3% of
   pairs on mazes. A multi-resolution clock the model sets per task, rather than one fixed by the designer.

6. **Thinking time gives calibrated, adaptive compute.** ImageNet-1K: 72.47% top-1 (ResNet-152 backbone, uncropped), not
   state of the art and not the goal; certainty rises over ticks, easy images stop early, and calibration is unusually
   good. CIFAR-100: two separate peaks of certainty over ticks — two internal processes depending on the input.

## What it does NOT show (for the notes' purpose)

- **Its time is INTERNAL, not the order of the data.** The notes' central claim is about the order of OBSERVATIONS as
  evidence about the generating rule (the pencil marks). The CTM thinks over time about data that mostly has no order
  (images, mazes, parity given all at once). It shows time is a useful COMPUTATIONAL resource and representation; it does
  not test whether ordered observations make rule induction easier.
- **Not data-efficient, and no help against grokking.** Mazes train for ~1,000,000 iterations, parity for ~200,000 with
  large variance across seeds. Nothing here speaks to learning the rule sooner.
- **The obstacle the notes name is real here.** Training backpropagates through every tick; the stated limitation is
  sequential processing that cannot be parallelised, plus the extra parameters of the neuron-level models (D × (M·H + H)).
  DiffusionBlocks (`docs/extra papers/DiffusionBlocks.pdf`) removes backpropagation through a recurrent-depth model's
  iterations by giving each its own denoising target; whether that carries over to CTM ticks is untested (my
  extrapolation).

## For DistributionPrediction

The CTM is evidence for three of the notes' premises — time as a first-class quantity is useful; a model need not be
given its coordinates if it can build them by moving; and representations built from statistics of a trajectory work
better than snapshots. It is not evidence about the notes' main proposal (an objective that predicts the extrapolated
distribution) or about data efficiency. A CTM-like substrate is a reasonable candidate for the model; the objective
remains to be designed.
