**Fig 4.** *[A frame in which the same horseshoe, marked "1" at its start, continues into a much larger curling shape
that sweeps up and around to the right; a small box at the bottom is labelled "OOD".]* — beside it, with an arrow:

In-distribution prediction obviously is easy. But if we extend this geometric intuition to out-of-distribution, we
have a problem that without a distribution out there we cannot just guess the next data point geometrically. In fig 3,
our distribution looks like a horseshoe. But in fig 4, we extend the exact same pattern OOD and we get something very
different from a horseshoe.

Without having enough data, we cannot possibly know from fig 3 that the real pattern is fig 4. However, we can know it
is possible, along with other functions, for instance, it is equally likely that fig 3's distribution extrapolates to
a donut.

We cannot enumerate over every discrete possibility of what the true function governing the shape of the distribution
is because they are infinite, even at fig 4, where we cannot even truly know this pattern continues forever, or that we
fully see how it curves. But we CAN guess the functions and their probability of being true based on current evidence,
and extrapolate.

**A research idea:** Instead of "predict the next token given the distribution", what if a model's objective function
was "extrapolate the best distribution for this function"? Though, it would require a domain and
dimensionality-agnostic thing to output to make those extrapolations, and then finally use the extrapolated
distribution to predict the next token.
