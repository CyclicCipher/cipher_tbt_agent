# A3: numbers of several digits, and place value (2026-10-29)

**The milestone** (ARCHITECTURE.md §6):
- **The task:** base-10 addition of numbers of several digits, every digit a random SDR.
- **The test:** numbers 2 digits longer than trained, ≥ 0.95 exact.

**The direction** (§7): place value as the group's chain of quotients (part 4, finding 3).

**The user's instructions** (2026-10-29): do A3 after the performance step, without needless pauses. This note is the
design behind LAB part 11 and what follows it. Nothing past part 11 is built.

## 1. What place value is, as structure

- **Two-digit numerals are coordinates on a chain of subgroups.** Take the numbers mod 100, written as two digits, the
  chain 100Z ⊂ 10Z ⊂ Z.
  - **The units digit** is the image in the quotient Z/10Z, n mod 10: a homomorphism. Adding numbers adds units mod
    10.
  - **The tens digit** is the coordinate in the subgroup 10Z/100Z ≅ Z/10, after the units are taken off. It is not a
    homomorphism of n.
  - **The carry:** Z/100 is not Z/10 × Z/10. It is a non-split extension of Z/10 by Z/10, and the carry is the
    extension's cocycle: tens add mod 10, plus 1 when the units overflow.
- **Length generalization is the chain getting longer.** Z/1000 adds one more factor to the same chain: 1000Z ⊂ 100Z
  ⊂ 10Z ⊂ Z.
  - **What carries over:** each factor is the same Z/10, joined to the next by the same cocycle.
  - **So:** a brain that learned "a digit position is a factor, and the carry joins neighbours" has learned something
    that holds at every length.
- **Base 10 is not a prime power.** Z/100 ≅ Z/4 × Z/25 (the Chinese remainder theorem).
  - **What v2 would find:** its coordinate step (part 4: characters kept in increasing order while they split places)
    would find the chain (2, 4, 5, 25), the residues mod 4 and 25, not the decimal digits.
  - **Why:** the decimal chain is one chain of subgroups among several. What singles it out is the notation, which
    digits are written where.
  - **Part 4's contrast:** in its Z/81 world, the 3-adic chain (3, 9, 27, 81) was also the coarsest by order.

## 2. What v2 has, and lacks, for it

| needed | v2 today |
|---|---|
| a number's value as a coordinate | the result places of "a + b" get a coordinate of Z/100 (an orbit of 100), as in A1 |
| the decimal chain | not found: the chain kept is by character order (CRT residues), not by what the numerals say |
| a digit's shift in each position | part 6's shift classes: the units digit d shifts by d, the tens digit by 10d. The relation between them is ×10, an endomorphism, not an automorphism (part 6 relates classes only by units k) |
| naming an answer of two digits | part 5's naming names a coordinate by one symbol (the one whose own position it is); no symbol names a two-digit value |
| the carry | nothing: no state carries from one digit position to the next |
| longer numbers | nothing: a numeral of 3 digits is a different world (Z/1000) |

## 3. The plan

1. **Part 11 (step 1): measure.**
   - **The world:** two-digit numerals mod 100, with no change to the brain.
   - **What it finds out:** what the current machinery learns of them: held-out answers; the coordinates and their
     chain; the shift classes per digit position; answers never seen as answers.
   - **What it's for:** it puts numbers on the table in §2, before any mechanism is designed.
2. **Step 2: naming by quotient.**
   - **The idea:** an answer digit's position names an image of the result's coordinate. For the units, the
     quotient: the content after "= c₁" is constant on the cosets of a subgroup of the coordinate group (10Z/100Z), and
     each coset is named by the digit seen there.
   - **What it predicts:** that subgroup is found from the data, as the coarsest one the content is constant on. It
     generalizes to results never seen as answers, whenever their coset was seen.
   - **What it doesn't do:** it does not give the tens digit (not a homomorphism).
3. **Step 3: the decimal chain from the notation.**
   - **The idea:** the subgroup found in step 2 fixes the first factor. The tens digit is then the coordinate in that
     subgroup, the result's coordinate minus its units' coset representative.
   - **The candidate:** extensions as composition, "the chain of quotients" made operational. Not designed.
4. **Step 4: the carry, and length.**
   - **The idea:** digit-by-digit addition as a chain (parts 9–10), with the carry as a held value.
   - **The open question:** whether the per-factor structure learned at 2 digits transfers to 4. This needs the
     working-memory machinery to hold a carry, and numerals of varying length in one world. Not designed.

**Why step 1 first:** each later step answers a gap in §2. Step 1 checks that the gaps are where §2 puts them, and
measures them, which is the user's "verify the mechanism first".

## 4. What step 1 found (LAB part 11), and what it means for step 2

- **Confirmed:**
  - held-out 1.000;
  - the CRT chain (2, 4, 5, 25);
  - tens shift = 10 × units shift, with no relation;
  - no naming;
  - withheld answers 0.000.
- **New:** without every result, there is no coordinate at all.
  - **Why:** one position's digit moves don't wrap (the wrap is the carry), so they are paths, not cycles. v2's
    coordinates need complete cycles (part 4).
- **Step 2 therefore has two parts:**
  1. **coordinates for a path:** a position's digits as a segment that closes into Z/10 only through the carry;
  2. **joining positions:** the carry as the link between neighbouring positions (the extension).

**What the brain does** (a short search, 2026-10-29):
- **Two-digit numbers are represented decomposed.** Tens and units are compared in separate bins: the unit-decade
  compatibility effect ([Nuerk, Weger & Willmes 2001, *Cognition* 82](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/4401)). The follow-ups
  find it in other presentations, and in English speakers, so it isn't specific to one language.
- **The carry is a working-memory and updating operation.** It holds the unit sum's decade digit and adds it to the
  next position. Carry problems recruit prefrontal cortex, beyond the parietal number system
  ([Klein et al. 2010](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3002291/)).
- **So the brain's structure matches §1:**
  - each position is its own coordinate (a decomposed, per-position code);
  - the carry is a held value moved between positions (the thalamus's working memory, part 10).

**Withdrawn (the user, 2026-10-29).** The three choices below were each shaped to get place value out of the learner:
per-position coordinates, naming by quotient, a carry slot, a change of notation. That is cargo-culting, by the
general-learner standard now in ARCHITECTURE.md §0.
- **What A3 is now:** a measurement of the general learner, given the data a human learning multi-digit addition
  would get.
- **The test:** does it learn as well as that human?
- **Where it falls short:** look for general architectural improvements, justified across worlds, not by A3.

They are kept below as the record of the mistake.

**Step 2's choices** (withdrawn):
- **(a) Coordinates per position, the carry learned as a held value.** Each position's digits form Z/10, made
  complete by the hypothesis that a path is a cycle cut by a carry: generalize, then correct. The carry is a working
  memory item that the gate learns to release into the next position. Closest to the brain's decomposed code. It
  needs the coordinate step to accept paths, and the gate to act between positions.
- **(b) Naming by quotient first.** Each answer position is named by the result coordinate's image in a learned
  quotient. It gives the units digit of unseen answers at once, but not the tens digit, and not the withheld arm,
  which has no coordinate. Smaller, but it stops short.
- **(c) Least significant digit first.** Change the world's notation so the carry runs forward in time. Addition
  becomes a left-to-right chain (parts 9–10's machinery), at the cost of a notation humans don't use.

**My recommendation is (a):** it is what the brain does, and it is the general structure (an extension built from one
factor and one link) that would carry over to longer numerals.
