What Gemini and I did here:

1. Applied the concept of Residuals from ResNets to symbolic function-finding. 
It exponentially shrank the problem space and difficulty of credit assignment, making it drastically more efficient to find symbolic functions to fit data.

2. Springboarded from that to an alternate way to make Thousand Brains Theory work, at a test scale.
Important is the idea of symbolic ResNets as fast, cheap "wind tunnels" for future architectures. Makes testing fast.
Previous experiments with TBT tried to replicate the literal wiring of the brain with apical, basal, proximal dendrites.
But at a small scale, the attention mechanism proved perfectly workable as a simple alternative.
The hypothesis was that a single network is always trying to learn a single dataset, hence the need for replay buffers to train AI.
Thousand Brains Theory is not a single network, so it would IN THEORY not need a replay buffer.
Hypothesis seems to be more or less proven.

3. The next work was the creation of a very basic ARC-AGI 3 replica designed to force us to evolve the architecture into something more intelligent.
The idea was: Combine DreamCoder's Bayesian approach, with symbolic ResNet, with the TBT approach above, to solve these games.
Percepta's Spotlight architecture was used to build a hippocampus to solve memory.

Lessons:
-Additive residual error fitting turns an exponential search (O(B^d)) into a linear one (O(d*B)).
(Where B = the number of vocabulary items, and d = the number of layers/terms in the function.)
-Replacing the residuals above with attention residuals not only vastly decreases the error, it results in learned functions much closer to the ground truth.
-Adelic, rather than Euclidean distance, proved strangely powerful in solving some of the problems where symbolic attnRes's greediness caused it to
fall into a local minima.
-Therefore Adelic distance is likely extremely useful for search?
-A circular/adelic loss function groks modular arithmetic almost instantly, practically no train/test gap. Promising results in boolean logic too.
